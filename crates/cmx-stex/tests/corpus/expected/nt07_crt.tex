\textit{Claim.} There exists $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $k \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ and $k \equiv 3 \pmod{5}$.

\textit{Proof.} Since $\gcd(3, 5) = 1$, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem there is a unique $k \bmod 15$ with $k \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ and $k \equiv 3 \pmod{5}$.
Indeed $k = 8$ works: $8 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ and $8 \equiv 3 \pmod{5}$.
Hence there exists $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $k \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ and $k \equiv 3 \pmod{5}$, as required. \hfill$\square$
