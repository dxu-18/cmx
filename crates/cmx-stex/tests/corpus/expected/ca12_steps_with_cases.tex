Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
We show that $n^{2} \bmod 4$ is $0$ or $1$.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Step 1: Reduce to parity.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Case 1: Suppose $n$ is even.
        Then $n = 2k$ and $n^{2} = 4k^{2}$.
      \item Case 2: Suppose $n$ is odd.
        Then $n = 2k + 1$ and $n^{2} = 4(k^{2} + k) + 1$.
    \end{itemize}

    So $n^{2}$ leaves remainder $0$ or $1$ upon division by $4$.
  \item Step 2: Conclude.
    $n^{2} \bmod 4$ is $0$ or $1$.
\end{itemize}

Hence $n^{2} \bmod 4$ is $0$ or $1$, as required. \hfill$\square$
