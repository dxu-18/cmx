Let $F_{0} = 0$, let $F_{1} = 1$, and let $F_{n + 1} = F_{n} + F_{n - 1}$ for $n \geq 1$.

\textit{Theorem.} For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $F_{n} < 2^{n}$.

\textit{Proof.} We proceed by strong induction on $n$.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Case 1: Suppose $n = 0$ or $n = 1$.
    Then $F_{n} \leq 1 < 2^{n}$.
  \item Case 2: Suppose $n \geq 2$.
    Then $F_{n} = F_{n - 1} + F_{n - 2} < 2^{n - 1} + 2^{n - 2} < 2^{n}$ by the inductive hypothesis.
\end{itemize}

Hence for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $F_{n} < 2^{n}$, as required. \hfill$\square$
