Let $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
We show that $n^{2} \equiv 0$ or $1 \bmod 4$.
Since $n$ is either even or odd, the following cases are exhaustive.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Case 1: Suppose $n$ is even.
    Then $n = 2m$, so $n^{2} = 4m^{2} \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$.
  \item Case 2: Suppose $n$ is odd.
    Then $n = 2m + 1$, so $n^{2} = 4(m^{2} + m) + 1 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.
\end{itemize}

In all exhaustive cases, $n^{2} \equiv 0$ or $n^{2} \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.
Hence $n^{2} \equiv 0$ or $1 \bmod 4$, as required. \hfill$\square$
