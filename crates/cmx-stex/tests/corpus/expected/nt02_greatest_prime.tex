We show that there does not exist greatest prime.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that there is a largest prime number.
Let $P$ denote the largest prime number such that $A = \{p_{1}, p_{2}, \ldots, P\}$ is the set of all prime numbers.
Then construct a number $N$ such that $N = (p_{1} \times p_{2} \times \cdots \times P) + 1$.
Note that for every $p \in A$, $N \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ implies it will always leave a remainder of $1$.

Hence there does not exist greatest prime, as required. \hfill$\square$
