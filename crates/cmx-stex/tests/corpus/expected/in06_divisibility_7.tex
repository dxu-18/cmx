\textit{Theorem.} For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $7 \mid 8^{n} - 1$.

\textit{Proof.} We proceed by induction on $n$.

\textit{Base case} ($n = 0$). $8^{0} - 1 = 0$ is divisible by $7$.

\textit{Inductive step.} Suppose that $7 \mid 8^{k} - 1$.
Then $8^{k + 1} - 1 = 8(8^{k} - 1) + 7$.
Since $7$ divides both terms, $7 \mid 8^{k + 1} - 1$.
Hence for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $7 \mid 8^{n} - 1$, as required. \hfill$\square$
