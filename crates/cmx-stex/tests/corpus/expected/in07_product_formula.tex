\textit{Theorem.} For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\prod_{i=1}^{n} (1 + \frac{1}{i}) = n + 1$.

\textit{Proof.} We proceed by induction on $n$.

\textit{Base case} ($n = 0$). The empty product is $1$.

\textit{Inductive step.} Suppose that $\prod_{i=1}^{k} (1 + \frac{1}{i}) = k + 1$.
Then the product up to $k + 1$ is $(k + 1)(1 + \frac{1}{k + 1}) = k + 2$.
Hence for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\prod_{i=1}^{n} (1 + \frac{1}{i}) = n + 1$, as required. \hfill$\square$
