\textit{Claim.} For every $x \in \mathbb{Z}$, $x^{2} \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ or $x^{2} \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.

\textit{Proof.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Case 1: Suppose $x$ is even.
    Write $x = 2k$.
    Then $x^{2} = 4k^{2} \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$.
  \item Case 2: Suppose $x$ is odd.
    Write $x = 2k + 1$.
    Then $x^{2} = 4(k^{2} + k) + 1 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.
\end{itemize}

Hence for every $x \in \mathbb{Z}$, $x^{2} \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ or $x^{2} \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, as required. \hfill$\square$

\textit{Corollary.} No integer of the form $4m + 3$ is a sum of two squares.
