\textit{Theorem.} $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

\textit{Proof.} Assume for the sake of contradiction that $\sqrt{2}$ is rational.
Then there exist $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $q \neq 0$ and $\gcd(p, q) = 1$ such that $\sqrt{2} = \frac{p}{q}$.
Squaring, $2q^{2} = p^{2}$, so $p^{2}$ is even and hence $p$ is even.
Write $p = 2r$ for some $r \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Then $2q^{2} = 4r^{2}$, so $q^{2} = 2r^{2}$ and $q$ is even.
Thus $2$ divides both $p$ and $q$, contradicting $\gcd(p, q) = 1$.
Hence $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, as required. \hfill$\square$
