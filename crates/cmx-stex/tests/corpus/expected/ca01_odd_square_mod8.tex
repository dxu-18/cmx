We show that $x$ is odd implies $8 \mid x^{2} - 1$.

Suppose that $x$ is odd.
Then there exists $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $x = 2k + 1$.
Then
\begin{align*}
  x^{2} - 1 &= (2k + 1)^{2} - 1 \\
  &= (4k^{2} + 4k + 1) - 1 \\
  &= 4k^{2} + 4k
\end{align*}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Case 1: Suppose $k$ is odd.
    Then there exists $l \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $k = 2l + 1$.
    Then
    \begin{align*}
      4k^{2} + 4k &= 4(2l + 1)^{2} + 4(2l + 1) \\
      &= 4(4l^{2} + 4l + 1) + 4(2l + 1) \\
      &= 8(2l^{2} + 2l) + 8 + 8l \\
      &= 8(2l^{2} + 3l + 1)
    \end{align*}

    Note that $(2l^{2} + 3l + 1) \in \mathbb{Z}$, so by definition of divisibility, $8 \mid (x^{2} - 1)$.
  \item Case 2: Suppose $k$ is even.
    Then there exists $l \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $k = 2l$.
    Then $4k^{2} + 4k = 4 \cdot 4l^{2} + 8l = 8(2l^{2} + l)$.

    Note that $(2l^{2} + l) \in \mathbb{Z}$, so by definition of divisibility $8 \mid (x^{2} - 1)$.
\end{itemize}

In all exhaustive cases, $8 \mid (x^{2} - 1)$, and thus $x$ is odd implies $8 \mid (x^{2} - 1)$.

Hence $x$ is odd implies $8 \mid x^{2} - 1$, as required. \hfill$\square$
