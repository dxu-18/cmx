Let $n$ be an integer.
We show that $n^{3} - n$ is divisible by $2$.
The following cases are exhaustive.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Case 1: Suppose $n$ is even.
    Then $n^{3}$ and $n$ are both even, so $n^{3} - n$ is even.
  \item Case 2: Suppose $n$ is odd.
    Then $n^{3}$ and $n$ are both odd, so $n^{3} - n$ is even.
\end{itemize}

Hence $n^{3} - n$ is divisible by $2$, as required. \hfill$\square$
