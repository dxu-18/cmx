\textit{Theorem.} For all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every $x \geq -1$, $(1 + x)^{n} \geq 1 + nx$.

\textit{Proof.} Let $x \geq -1$.
We proceed by induction on $n$.

\textit{Base case} ($n = 0$). $(1 + x)^{0} = 1 = 1 + 0x$.

\textit{Inductive step.} Suppose that $(1 + x)^{k} \geq 1 + kx$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$.
Then $(1 + x)^{k + 1} = (1 + x)^{k}(1 + x) \geq (1 + kx)(1 + x)$ since $1 + x \geq 0$.
And $(1 + kx)(1 + x) = 1 + (k + 1)x + kx^{2} \geq 1 + (k + 1)x$.
Hence for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every $x \geq -1$, $(1 + x)^{n} \geq 1 + nx$, as required. \hfill$\square$
