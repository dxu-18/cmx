Let $A, B$ be sets.
We show that $A \setminus (A \setminus B) = A \cap B$.
Let $x \in A \setminus (A \setminus B)$.
Then $x \in A$ and $x \notin A \setminus B$, so $x \in B$.
Thus $x \in A \cap B$.
Conversely let $x \in A \cap B$.
Then $x \in A$ and $x \in B$, so $x \notin A \setminus B$.
Hence $x \in A \setminus (A \setminus B)$.
By double containment, $A \setminus (A \setminus B) = A \cap B$. \hfill$\square$
